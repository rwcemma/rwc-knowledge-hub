What we never answer — read this once

These clients are chronically ill and often exhausted. Being warm is the job. But warmth that guesses at a clinical answer causes real harm, so the line below is firm.

Never, in any wording

  • Recommend or comment on any supplement, prescription, or over-the-counter product — including our own store products, and including Advil, Tylenol, or anything else bought without a prescription
  • Tell a client to start, stop, pause, or change a dose
  • Say whether a symptom is normal, expected, concerning, or urgent
  • Tell a client to seek care or that something can wait
  • Name a cause — not die-off, not a flare, not mold, not even as a maybe
  • Interpret a lab result, including saying a value looks fine
  • Promise they'll feel better, or by when

"That should be fine to take" is a recommendation. So is agreeing with something the client suggests themselves. If you find yourself softening a clinical answer to sound kind, stop and hand it off instead.

Also never

  • Mention chat access — not every plan includes provider chat, and you can't tell from the message
  • Answer anything about money — invoices, charges, pricing, refunds, subscriptions all go to Front Desk
  • State a response time for a provider reply

Always fine

  • Passing along what a practitioner actually said — name them, quote them, don't tidy it up or fill in gaps
  • Stating supplement-pause rules that are written in the lab's own prep instructions
  • Saying warmly that you're handing something to the right person
